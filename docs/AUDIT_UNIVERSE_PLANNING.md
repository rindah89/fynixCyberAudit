# Audit universe and risk-based planning

Fynix Cyber Audit provides a deliberately maintained auditable-entity universe and ordinal risk-based audit-planning foundation. It complements the existing Program, Audit, AuditItem, data-request, finding, and remediation workflows; it does not automatically discover the organization or optimize audit resources.

## Auditable entities

Users with `Update Programs` register and update auditable entities through REST. Each record has a unique code, name, optional description, type, accountable owner, criticality, lifecycle status, assessment cadence, next-assessment date, and at least one mapped risk; mapped controls are optional. Types are `business_unit`, `business_process`, `legal_entity`, `technology`, `third_party`, `program`, and `other`. Criticality is `low`, `medium`, `high`, or `critical`.

The assigned owner or a user with `Update Programs` records an assessment. Likelihood and impact values use the existing 1–5 ordinal convention. The server derives inherent and residual 1–25 scores, rejects residual exposure above inherent exposure, and derives a deterministic priority band. Each append-only assessment snapshots the entity plus material mapped risk/control values, actor/time, rationale, next assessment date, and a SHA-256 fingerprint. Once assessment history exists, ordinary entity maintenance cannot move that due date; a new assessment is required. A material entity, risk, control, or mapping change produces `reassessment_required`; a passed due date produces `assessment_overdue`.

Priority is `critical` for residual score 20–25 or critical entities scoring at least 15; `high` for residual score 15–19 or any remaining critical entity; `medium` for residual score 8–14 or a high-criticality entity; otherwise `low`. These bands are transparent ordinal prioritization, not calibrated probability or quantitative loss.

## Risk-based audit plans

Users with `Update Programs` create a draft plan for a calendar year and assign a manager. The plan manager or a user with `Update Programs` adds an entity only with its current, non-stale assessment. Item status is `planned`, `scheduled`, or `deferred`; `scheduled` requires a linked existing `audit_id`. Planned dates must be inside the plan year. The server snapshots the complete assessment evidence and derives `priority_rank` as priority-band weight × 100 plus residual score.

Draft item dates, rationale, status, and optional audit link can be corrected, and a draft item can be removed. Approval requires at least one item and re-locks every entity, assessment, mapping, mapped risk/control, and linked audit to prove the evidence is still current. The server orders items by derived rank, records approver/time, snapshots the plan and every item, fingerprints that snapshot, and makes the approved plan and its items immutable through product interfaces. Changes after approval require a new plan record.

## Access and evidence surfaces

Users with `Read Programs` or `Update Programs` inspect the complete universe and plans. Entity owners see their assigned entities and assessment history; plan managers see their plans and items. **Foundations → Audit Universe** and **Foundations → Risk-Based Audit Plans** provide read-only, paginated inspection and relation-scoped private exports. REST lists are paginated with a maximum page size of 100.

## Approved-plan engagement handoff

After approval, a planned item without an existing audit can launch one audit engagement through REST. The caller must have `Create Audits` and must be either the approved plan manager or have `Update Programs`. The request supplies the audit title/type, accountable manager, optional program and description, explicit objective/scope/exclusions, and up to 100 active team members. The server uses the approved item dates, starts the audit in `Not Started`, ensures the manager is on the team, and creates the audit and team membership in the same transaction.

The handoff does not rewrite the approved plan item. Instead, it creates one immutable engagement baseline linking the new audit to the approved item. The baseline retains the plan approval identity/fingerprint, item priority and dates, complete entity-assessment snapshot, objective, scope, exclusions, sorted team identities, launcher/time, and a SHA-256 content fingerprint. The audit detail REST response, operator audit detail, and audit export expose that baseline. Subsequent audit execution may update the operational audit, but it does not rewrite what was authorized at launch.

## Governed engagement closeout

Approved-plan engagements use a two-step closeout instead of the legacy direct-completion action. The accountable audit manager or an `Update Audits` user submits a versioned closeout only while the audit is in progress. Every audit item must be complete with auditor notes, an effectiveness conclusion, and—for controls—an applicability conclusion. Every data request must be accepted or rejected. A closeout is bounded to 250 audit items and 500 data requests, and captured item notes/request details are limited to 30,000 characters each.

The submission records an opinion (`satisfactory`, `needs_improvement`, or `unsatisfactory`), executive summary, optional scope limitations, significant matters, and recommendations. The server locks and snapshots the audit, approved engagement baseline, current team identities, every audit item plus governed control/implementation identity and conclusion, and every data-request status. It assigns the next version, submitter/time, and a reproducible SHA-256 fingerprint. While a submission awaits review, the audit scope, items, and data requests are frozen through product interfaces.

An independent user with `Update Audits`—not the audit manager or submitter—records `approved` or `rejected` with a review summary. Rejection retains both records and unfreezes fieldwork for a new submission version. Approval re-locks and revalidates the captured audit, team, item, request, control, and implementation snapshots before it creates a private final PDF, records its snapshotted storage disk/path, actual size, SHA-256, reviewer/time, complete report snapshot, and review fingerprint, then marks the audit complete. An approved governed audit cannot be reopened or deleted through ordinary product interfaces. The audit workspace provides paginated closeout history, complete inspection, and private relation export. Its report action and the governed download route both verify the retained size and SHA-256 before returning report bytes and reject changed content. REST is the maintenance interface for submission/review; the operator workspace is inspection and verified report retrieval.

## Governed work program

While an audit is in progress and not submitted for closeout, its manager or an `Update Audits` user deliberately defines versioned procedures against an existing audit item. Each definition records code, title, objective, steps, test method, optional population and planned sample, a current audit-team assignee, an optional due date within the audit period, creator, and time. Fynix allocates the version; a later version of the same code can be defined only after the current version is executed.

The assigned auditor, audit manager, or an `Update Audits` user executes a procedure once with an outcome, result, optional exceptions, tested-sample count, and optional unverified evidence reference. The server prevents a tested count above the planned sample, retains the complete procedure/item/auditable snapshot plus executor/time and SHA-256 fingerprint, and makes the execution append-only through product interfaces. All defined procedures must be executed before governed closeout; their complete execution snapshots become part of the closeout and final-report evidence. Paginated REST and operator history, complete inspection, and private relation export expose the work program.

## Governed effort and time

While the engagement remains in progress and outside closeout freeze, the audit manager or an `Update Audits` user records versioned effort budgets for a current team member at audit or assigned-procedure level. Each version retains planned minutes, rationale, the audit/procedure/user allocation snapshot, setter/time, and a SHA-256 fingerprint. Current team members record their own dated time against the audit or a procedure assigned to them; the server retains the current budget and procedure snapshots, activity, optional notes and unverified source reference, entrant/time, and fingerprint. Entries are append-only through product interfaces. Corrections create one attributable reversal rather than rewriting history.

The server derives planned, active actual, and variance minutes from the latest allocation versions and unreversed work entries. Budget history is bounded to 2,000 versions per audit, time history to 10,000 entries including reversals, and active time to 1,440 minutes per person/day. Paginated REST and operator history, complete inspection, private relation exports, and the governed closeout/report snapshot expose the retained evidence and summary.

## Limitations

All entities, mappings, scores, rationales, charter content, effort budgets, time entries, procedure definitions/results, fieldwork conclusions, closeout narratives, team selection, launch, submission, and review actions are deliberate operator inputs. Fynix does not discover entities, ingest risk telemetry, calculate calibrated likelihood or financial loss, optimize staffing/budgets, capture time automatically, provide payroll or billing, forecast capacity/utilization or assurance coverage, generate procedures, select samples, allocate resources automatically, or automatically launch, review, or execute audits. Evidence references are unverified text. Snapshot/report hashes establish retained content identity, not the truth, sufficiency, assurance quality, sampling validity, signature identity proofing, or external authenticity of the underlying assessment, charter, fieldwork, or report. Storage/database administrators remain outside product-interface immutability and retained-object guarantees. Legacy audits not launched from an approved plan retain their existing completion workflow and are not covered by the governed-closeout claim.
