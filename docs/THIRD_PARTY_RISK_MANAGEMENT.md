# Third-party risk management foundation

Fynix provides a governed third-party risk workflow on top of its existing vendor inventory, surveys, documents, portal, scoring, and exports. The foundation is narrower than continuous third-party monitoring or a complete supplier lifecycle platform.

## Operator workflow

Users with `Manage Third Party Risk` can record a versioned vendor risk assessment through REST. A vendor relationship manager can discover and inspect only their assigned record in the read-only **Risk Management → Third-Party Risk** workspace but cannot create governance records unless separately authorized. That dedicated workspace shows assessment history, linked risks, decision history, and review history without exposing unrelated vendor administration.

An assessment uses the standard 1–5 likelihood and impact scales. The server derives inherent and residual scores, allocates the next version, and may snapshot a completed, scored `vendor_assessment` survey belonging to the same vendor. The survey remains the governed due-diligence source; its score and scoring timestamp are required but are not recalculated by the assessment workflow.

Before a decision, the operator links at least one existing risk classified as `third_party`. Approved or conditionally approved decisions snapshot the assessment version, residual score, linked risk identities, and governance fingerprint. A new assessment or current vendor/risk values that differ from that snapshot require a new decision. Mutable vendor records do not provide permanent field-level change history; reverting to the approved snapshot restores the prior derived state.

Periodic reviews are attributable to both the reviewer and the approved decision baseline. An authorized reviewer may deliberately bind one to 20 accepted audit-evidence attachments they can access. Fynix retains bounded copies and snapshots the attachment/response/request/audit identities, accepted state, metadata, actual size, disk/path, SHA-256, reviewer, and time. Downloads reauthorize both the vendor workspace and exact linked attachment; unauthorized viewers receive no evidence count, action, metadata, or content. `needs_action` and `terminate` outcomes open persistent third-party risk issues. Later satisfactory reviews do not hide open issues. Assessments, decisions, reviews, and linked evidence are append-only through product interfaces. Evidence references remain operator-supplied, unverified external text.

Managers can also record immutable, versioned fourth-party dependency declarations for a primary vendor. A declaration identifies either another vendor record or a normalized external organization name, affected business service, service category and description, criticality, data-access indicator, lifecycle state, rationale, and optional source reference. The server snapshots the primary vendor, fourth party, affected service, and material dependency values. Recording another version with `exited` state removes that vendor/dependency pair from current concentration without rewriting history.

Managers can govern an internal vendor engagement from proposal through due diligence, independent approval, activation, renewal review, and exit. A proposal retains its business owner, service, criticality, data-access declaration, term, review date, proposer, and complete vendor snapshot. Approval requires the latest unexpired approved or conditionally approved vendor-risk decision against the exact current assessment and risk graph. The approver must differ from the proposer, current assessor, and risk-decision actor.

The due-diligence phase requires a structured review before approval. A separated reviewer selects a completed, scored vendor-assessment survey and may select up to 20 current approved vendor documents they are authorized to view; records five one-to-five domain ratings, findings, decision, conditions, rationale, and an in-term next-review date; and binds complete bounded survey evidence, authorized document metadata, the exact engagement event, current vendor-risk approval, actor/time, and SHA-256 fingerprint. A satisfactory or conditional latest review is required for approval, and its reviewer cannot approve the engagement. History is capped at 100 versions and exposed through scoped paginated REST and read-only operator inspection; survey and document snapshots are projected only when the current viewer still passes the exact source-record ACL.

Before initial or renewed activation, a further separated reviewer records a contract-risk judgment against the exact engagement event and current vendor-risk approval. It retains the operator-supplied agreement reference/type and dates; confidentiality, data-protection, incident-notification, audit-right, subcontractor, continuity, and termination-assistance indicators; service-level, liability, exit, exception, condition, and rationale text; complete engagement/risk snapshots; actor/time; and SHA-256 fingerprint. Unqualified approval requires every governed term indicator; conditional approval requires conditions. The reviewed agreement dates must cover an initial retained term, and a renewed review must cover the proposed extended term. Changed risk or engagement approval context requires attributable due-diligence reapproval and a new contract review. Activation also checks the retained term start. Exit retains the deliberate exit summary and data-disposition statement. All lifecycle and contract review evidence is exposed through scoped paginated REST and operator history.

Before initial activation, managers define up to 100 immutable onboarding controls with category, acceptance criteria, active owner, due date, required flag, complete engagement snapshot, actor/time, and fingerprint. The owner or a manager may append up to ten attributable completion versions with deliberate summary and unverified source reference. An independent reviewer separated from engagement, contract, control-owner, and completion actors records one of up to 100 readiness decisions against the complete current requirement/latest-completion set and exact engagement/contract fingerprints. Every required control needs completion evidence; conditional readiness requires conditions; activation requires a current ready or ready-with-conditions decision and a different activation actor. Scoped paginated REST and read-only operator history expose the retained evidence.

While an engagement is active, a third-party risk manager can define immutable, versioned monitoring indicators against the latest accepted contract review and exact current vendor-risk approval. Each definition retains code, name, category, unit, adverse direction, fixed-decimal warning and critical thresholds, one-to-366-day cadence, active owner, deliberate measurement method, complete engagement/contract/risk snapshots, actor/time, and SHA-256 fingerprint. A manager, indicator owner, or engagement business owner appends fixed-decimal observations with source reference, notes, exact nested snapshots, actor/time, and fingerprint. The server derives normal, warning, critical, awaiting-observation, overdue, and action-required display state. Only the latest definition for a code can receive observations; changed vendor-risk or contract context requires a new accepted review and definition. Definitions are limited to 100 versions per engagement and observations to 1,000 per definition. Scoped paginated REST exposes complete retained history; read-only operator inspection exposes every definition and its latest ten observations, with older observations remaining available through REST.

Current concentration is derived from the latest declaration for each primary-vendor/dependency pair. `limited`, `moderate`, and `high` bands use the number of distinct primary vendors plus the number of high/critical dependencies. Assigned vendor managers can inspect only their vendor's complete history and see an aggregate count/band without other vendor identities. Users with `Manage Third Party Risk` or `Read Vendors` can inspect cross-vendor concentration, including the contributing primary vendors. The operator relation history is paginated and exportable to authorized readers.

Before exit, managers define up to 100 immutable offboarding controls with category, acceptance criteria, active owner, due date, required flag, material active-engagement snapshot, actor/time, and fingerprint. Owners or managers append up to ten attributable completion versions with deliberate summary and unverified source reference. A reviewer separated from engagement, contract, control-owner, and completion actors records one of up to 100 readiness decisions against every current control/latest completion and the exact active engagement event. Every required control needs completion evidence; conditional readiness requires conditions; exit requires a current ready or ready-with-conditions decision and a different exit approver. Scoped paginated REST and read-only operator history expose the retained evidence.

During due diligence, approval, active service, or renewal review, a manager may open up to 100 governed collaboration requests to an exact activated contact for the engagement vendor. Each request retains category, subject, deliberate request text, due date, material engagement and recipient snapshots, opener/time, version, and SHA-256 fingerprint. Only that authenticated vendor-portal recipient may append a response, optional unverified source reference, and up to 20 same-vendor documents currently visible to them. Selected content is copied under runtime limits of 10 MiB per file and 50 MiB per response; retained provenance, actual size, disk/path, and SHA-256 are bound into the event fingerprint. A manager separated from the request opener may accept the response or request follow-up; the same recipient can answer the follow-up. Each request retains at most 20 immutable events with complete request, actor, status, content, source, summary, time, version, and fingerprint evidence. Staff use scoped paginated REST/read-only operator history; recipients see only their exact assigned requests and response action in the vendor portal. File metadata and exact retained-copy downloads independently require current collaboration-workspace and source-document ACL.

While a request awaits a provider response and has not escalated, a manager may append at most 20 recipient reassignments to another activated contact for the same vendor after any pending due-date extension receives a terminal decision. The original request and recipient remain immutable. Each reassignment retains the exact prior and replacement recipient snapshots, request context, reason, manager/time, version, and fingerprint. Current portal discovery, response authority, evidence download, extension requests, future reminder delivery, and escalation use the latest reassigned recipient. A former recipient immediately loses current request visibility and action/download authority; reassignment removes that recipient's mutable bell notifications for the request while preserving the immutable delivery ledger. Staff REST/read-only operator history exposes complete reassignment evidence; the current exact recipient sees provider-safe reassignment history without internal actor or request snapshots.

While the request awaits that recipient and has not escalated, the exact recipient may submit at most 20 immutable due-date extension proposals. Each proposal must extend the current effective due date without exceeding the engagement term and retains the original request, recipient, prior due context, reason, time, version, and fingerprint. A third-party risk manager separated from the request opener approves or rejects only the latest pending proposal, retaining the complete proposal, actor, summary, time, and fingerprint. Approval does not rewrite the original deadline: it establishes a separately fingerprinted effective-due context. Rejection leaves the last approved context unchanged. Staff REST/read-only operator history and the exact-recipient portal expose the proposal and decision evidence.

A daily idempotent reconciliation sends at most one `due_soon` and one `overdue` in-app database notification per request, using the then-current effective date rather than rewriting the original request deadline. Due-soon begins in the inclusive three-day window and overdue begins after the effective date has ended, only while the latest request event still awaits that exact recipient. An extension is no longer available after overdue evidence exists. Notification insertion and immutable delivery evidence share the same transaction. Each new or backfilled record retains the exact due context, recipient, request and latest-event snapshots, type, channel, notification identity, attempted/delivered times, and SHA-256 fingerprint; deleting the bell notification does not delete delivery evidence. Staff REST/operator history and exact-recipient portal history expose the retained delivery records. This proves insertion into the vendor portal's database notification store, not human reading or external delivery.

A second daily idempotent reconciliation escalates a request once after three complete grace days beyond the exact effective due context, only when that context's governed overdue reminder exists and the latest event still awaits the provider. Under the vendor → engagement → request → provider-recipient → event → extension lock order, it resolves the current active engagement business owner and vendor relationship manager, deduplicates them when they are the same person, and atomically inserts database notifications plus one immutable escalation record. The record binds the effective due context, every internal recipient and accountability role, the exact request/latest event/overdue-reminder snapshots, notification identities, attempted/delivered times, and SHA-256 fingerprint. Staff REST/operator history exposes complete evidence; the exact provider recipient sees only that internal escalation occurred, its channel, delivery time, and fingerprint—not internal recipient identity. This proves in-app database insertion, not reading, external escalation, response resolution, or remediation.

The current business owner or vendor relationship manager may acknowledge that escalation with a summary, action plan, and target date. A different currently accountable or third-party-risk-authorized user may resolve it only after a later provider response has received an `accepted` decision, and must also differ from that acceptance actor. The server retains at most one immutable acknowledgement and one immutable resolution with complete escalation, actor, accepted-event, time, version, and SHA-256 evidence. Full evidence is available through scoped staff REST and read-only operator history; the exact provider recipient sees only the internal lifecycle status, time, and fingerprint. These records prove deliberate internal acknowledgement and resolution decisions, not human reading, provider-response truth, action-plan execution, remediation, external delivery, or provider performance.

After an acknowledged target date has fully ended while the request still awaits the provider, a third-party risk manager may deliberately open one idempotent high-severity governance issue from the REST or operator workspace. The issue fingerprints the exact escalation, acknowledgement, latest awaiting event, current active accountable owner, opener, rationale, and time, then enters the shared owner-scoped remediation and independent closure lifecycle with its due date set to the missed target. A later accepted response or internal escalation resolution does not close that issue; remediation handoff, task completion, verification, and accepted-file closure evidence remain separate deliberate actions. No automatic treatment selection, remediation execution, effectiveness inference, or closure is claimed.

## Derived status

The vendor workspace derives assessment-required, risk-link-required, decision-required, reapproval-required, approved, conditionally-approved, rejected, terminated, approval-expired, review-overdue, action-required, and termination-required states.

## Limitations

Collaboration records prove only attributable insertion, in-app database-notification delivery, and retained-byte identity in the authenticated in-product portal workflow; they do not prove external identity, human reading, email or external delivery, response truth, source authenticity, sufficiency, relevance, or provider performance. Database failures invoke compensating cleanup for uncommitted retained copies; storage-adapter and administrator failures remain outside that guarantee.

Evidence selection, dependency declarations, engagement facts, contract terms, indicator definitions, observed values, sources, and review conclusions remain deliberate operator/integration inputs. A SHA-256 hash proves retained-record identity, not truth, sufficiency, authenticity, legal effect, provider performance, compliance, or that a conclusion was derived from an executed agreement. Fourth-party identity and ownership are not externally verified. Contract-risk and engagement approval do not author, negotiate, sign, store, transmit, or execute a contract; perform procurement or technical onboarding; validate service delivery; prove data deletion; or execute offboarding. Monitoring does not ingest service telemetry, validate source reports, calculate service credits, infer performance/compliance, or create remediation automatically. This foundation does not provide supplier discovery, sanctions or financial feeds, attack-surface telemetry, ownership intelligence, regulatory content, full contract lifecycle management, assessment-evidence ingestion, automatic evidence collection, automatic reassessment, or automated remediation. Review issues use the separately documented governed remediation and independent-closure workflow with separate content-hashed accepted audit evidence. Vendor surveys and documents remain separately governed workflows. This is not a claim of complete ServiceNow third-party risk management parity.
